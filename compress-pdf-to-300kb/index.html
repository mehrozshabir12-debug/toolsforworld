Compress PDF to 300KB

Choose a PDF and it is made just under 300 KB, keeping as much quality as possible. Ideal for visa and admission forms that allow up to 300 KB.

How to compress a PDF to 300KB

  1. Choose your PDF.
  2. The target is already set to 300 KB. Change it if your form allows a different size.
  3. Press Compress PDF and download the smaller file.

How it works

Each page is re-saved as a compressed image. The tool tries several quality levels and keeps the sharpest one that still fits under 300 KB. Your file never leaves your device.

Tips

  • Scanned documents and PDFs full of photos shrink the most.
  • Fewer pages means better quality at 300 KB. Remove pages you don't need with Split PDF first.
  • Text in the result can no longer be selected or searched, which is fine for forms and scans.

What fits in 300 KB?

300 KB is common on visa and admission forms. It holds roughly three to six scanned pages: a short bank statement, a reference letter or a set of transcripts.

  • Remove blank pages and cover sheets before compressing.
  • Merge first, then compress: if a form wants all documents in one file, join them with Merge PDF and then set the target to 300 KB.
  • Check readability: zoom in on small print such as account numbers before you submit.

Questions

What if my PDF can't get under 300KB?

The tool shows the smallest size it could reach. Split the PDF into parts or remove pages, then try again.

Is my PDF uploaded?

No. Compression happens inside your browser, so the file stays on your device.

Should I merge before or after compressing?

Merge first, then compress the single file to the limit. Compressing pieces separately often ends up larger than the limit when joined.

Does this work for “below 300 KB”, “less than 300 KB” or “up to 300 KB” limits?

Yes. Under, below, less than and up to 300 KB all mean the same thing: the file must be 300 KB or smaller. The PDF is made just under 300 KB, so it passes any of these limits on upload forms.

Can I compress a PDF to 300 KB without losing quality?

For most documents the change is hard to see. Typed text stays sharp, and scans of one to five pages usually fit under 300 KB with readable text. Long scans with many photos may need splitting first.
