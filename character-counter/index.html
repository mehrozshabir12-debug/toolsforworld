Common character limits

  • X (Twitter) post: 280 characters.
  • SMS: 160 characters per message (70 with emoji or non-Latin letters).
  • Instagram bio: 150 characters.
  • Google meta description: about 155 characters before it gets cut off.
  • Google Ads headline: 30 characters; description: 90.

Emoji and many non-Latin letters count as one character here, the way people read them.

Why character limits differ between apps

Some platforms count every Unicode code unit, so an emoji or an Urdu, Arabic or Hindi letter with marks can count as two or more. Others count what you see. If a post is close to the limit, leave a few characters spare, and check the count shown in the app itself before posting.

Writing within a limit

  • Lead with the most important words: search results and previews cut off the end.
  • Replace long phrases with short ones (“in order to” → “to”).
  • Remove filler words like “really”, “very” and “just”.
  • Use numbers instead of words where it reads naturally.

Questions

Do spaces count as characters?

Usually yes. That's why both numbers are shown: with spaces and without spaces.

Is my text saved?

No. Counting happens in your browser and nothing is stored.

Are line breaks counted?

Yes. Line breaks count as characters, as they do on most platforms.

What is the limit for an SMS?

160 characters with basic Latin letters, or 70 when the message contains emoji or non-Latin script.
