How to compress a PDF

  1. Choose a PDF.
  2. Pick a level: Strong suits most scans, Light keeps more detail.
  3. Press Compress PDF and download the smaller file.

Good to know

Each page is re-saved as a compressed image. That is why scanned PDFs often get 60–90% smaller. Text in the result can no longer be selected or searched. If your PDF is mostly typed text, it is probably already small.

Which level should I pick?

  • Light: for documents people will read on screen or print, such as contracts or reports with photos. Keeps the most detail.
  • Recommended or Strong: for scanned forms, ID copies and receipts that only need to be readable. Usually the best balance for upload portals.
  • Extreme: only when a portal has a very small limit. Small print may become hard to read, so zoom in and check before you submit.

Why some PDFs barely shrink

A PDF made directly from Word or a website is mostly text and vector shapes, which are already tiny. Most of the size in a big PDF comes from photos and scans, and those are what this tool squeezes. If your file is text-only and still too large, it may contain embedded fonts or a few huge images; splitting it into parts with Split PDF can also help.

Need a fixed size? Use a preset such as 1 MB, 500 KB or 200 KB.

Questions

How much smaller will my PDF get?

Scanned and photo-heavy PDFs usually shrink by 50–90%. Text-only PDFs may not shrink.

Is there a file size limit?

No fixed limit. Very large PDFs (hundreds of pages) can be slow on phones.

Will compressing remove my signature or stamp?

No. Everything visible on the page is kept, only at a lower image quality.

Can I still search the text after compressing?

No. Pages become images, so the text is no longer selectable. Keep the original if you need searchable text.

Can I compress a PDF without uploading it?

Yes, that is how this tool works. The PDF is compressed by your own browser, on your device, and is never sent to a server. You can even turn off Wi-Fi after the page loads and it still works.
