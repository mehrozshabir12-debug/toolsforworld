How to compress an image

  1. Choose one or more photos.
  2. Set the quality (70% keeps photos sharp for most uses).
  3. Press Compress and save each smaller file.

Need a photo under a fixed size for an application form? Type it in Target size, or open a ready preset: 20 KB, 50 KB, 100 KB, 200 KB, 1 MB.

Need exact pixel dimensions instead (like 1080 × 1080)? Use the Image Resizer.

Quality vs. file size

JPG and WebP compression throws away detail the eye barely notices. Between 60% and 80% quality, most photos look the same on a phone screen but are several times smaller than the original. Below about 40% you start to see blocky patches, especially in skies and skin.

Which format compresses best?

  • JPG: best for photos; works everywhere.
  • WebP: often smaller than JPG at the same quality; good for websites.
  • PNG: lossless, so photos stay big. Screenshots and logos are fine as PNG; for photos convert with PNG to JPG first.

Typical targets

Exam and government forms often ask for photos under 20–100 KB and signatures under 10–30 KB. Email works best under 1–2 MB per photo, and website images load fastest under 200 KB.

Questions

Are my photos uploaded?

No. Compression happens inside your browser.

What if my image is already small?

If compressing would make the file bigger, the tool keeps your original and tells you, so you never get a larger "compressed" file.

Can I compress many photos at once?

Yes. Choose several files and download them one by one or as a ZIP.

Can I reduce image size without uploading the photo?

Yes. The photo is compressed by your browser on your own device and is never uploaded, which is safer for ID photos, signatures and documents.
