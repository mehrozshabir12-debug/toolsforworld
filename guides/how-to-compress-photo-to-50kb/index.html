How to reduce a photo to 50 KB for online forms

By Mehroz Shabir · Updated 15 October 2026

Exam, job and government portals often reject photos that are even 1 KB over the limit. Here is how to get under 50 KB (or any size) while keeping your face sharp.

Why phone photos are too big

A modern phone camera saves photos of 12 megapixels or more, usually 2–5 MB each. That is 40 to 100 times bigger than a 50 KB limit. To fit, a photo needs two things: fewer pixels and stronger compression. Done in the right order, the result still looks clear at the size the form shows it.

The quick way

  1. Open Compress Image to 50 KB.
  2. Choose your photo. The target is already set to 50 KB.
  3. Press Compress and download the result. The tool picks the best quality that stays just under 50 KB.

Other common limits: 20 KB, 30 KB, 100 KB, 200 KB. For any other number, type it into the Image Compressor.

The way that gives the best quality

  1. Read the form's rules first. Note the file size (for example 20–50 KB), the format (usually JPG) and any size in pixels or centimetres.
  2. Crop first. Cut away everything except your head and shoulders. Every pixel of wall you remove is quality saved for your face. If the form gives a size in cm, use the passport photo maker; if it gives pixels, use Resize Image.
  3. Then compress to the limit. Set the target to the maximum allowed, not lower, so you keep as much detail as possible and stay above any minimum.
  4. Check the result by opening it and zooming in on your eyes. If it looks blocky, crop tighter and try again.

Signatures under 10–20 KB

  • Sign with a dark blue or black pen on plain white paper.
  • Photograph it in daylight, straight from above, with no shadow from the phone.
  • Crop tightly around the signature, then compress to 10 KB or 20 KB. A tightly cropped signature stays sharp even at 10 KB.

KB, pixels and centimetres: what's the difference?

  • KB (kilobytes) is the file size: how much storage the photo takes. “Photo must be under 50 KB” is about this.
  • Pixels are the width and height of the image on screen, like 300 × 400 px.
  • Centimetres describe the printed size. At 300 dpi, 3.5 × 4.5 cm is about 413 × 531 pixels.

Many forms mix these up and say “resize to 50 KB” when they mean the file size. If the instruction ends in KB, you need the compressor, not the resizer.

Common reasons the form still says “file too large”

  • 1 KB = 1024 bytes. Some tools count 1 KB as 1000 bytes, so a “49 KB” file can be over the limit. Our compressor uses 1024, which strict portals expect.
  • Wrong format. The portal wants JPG but you uploaded PNG or HEIC. Convert with PNG to JPG or HEIC to JPG first.
  • Below the minimum. Some forms reject files that are too small, like under 20 KB. Set the target to the upper limit.
  • Old cached file. You uploaded the original instead of the compressed copy. Check the file name.

Is it safe for documents like ID cards?

Yes. Our tools run in your browser, so the photo is compressed on your own device and never uploaded to us. That matters for ID cards, certificates and signatures.

Questions

How do I compress a photo to 50 KB on my phone?

Open Compress Image to 50 KB in your phone's browser, choose the photo from your gallery and press Compress. No app is needed.

Will my photo look blurry at 50 KB?

Not if you crop it first. A head-and-shoulders photo stays clear at 50 KB; a full-size scene with lots of background looks worse.

What if the form asks for 20 to 50 KB?

Set the target to 50 KB. That keeps the most quality and stays above the 20 KB minimum.
