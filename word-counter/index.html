What it counts

  • Words and characters, with and without spaces.
  • Sentences and paragraphs.
  • Reading time at 238 words a minute and speaking time at 140.

Common limits

  • X (Twitter) post: 280 characters.
  • Meta description: about 155 characters.
  • College essays: often 250–650 words.

Common word limits

  • University essays often allow 10% above or below the set word count. Check your course rules.
  • Personal statements and cover letters are usually 400–650 words.
  • Abstracts are often limited to 150–300 words.

Your text stays in your browser and is never uploaded. Need a character limit instead? Use the character counter.

Typical word counts

  • University essay: often 1,500–3,000 words, usually with a ±10% allowance; check your unit guide.
  • Abstract: 150–300 words.
  • Cover letter: 250–400 words, about one page.
  • Blog post: 800–2,000 words.
  • Speech: about 130–150 words per minute of speaking.

How words are counted

Anything separated by spaces counts as a word, so “well-known” is one word and “U.S.” is one word. Numbers count as words. Different apps can disagree by a few words because of hyphens, dashes and symbols, so if a deadline system counts differently, use their number for the final check.

Need only characters, for a post or bio? Try the Character Counter.

Questions

Is my text saved?

No. The counting happens in your browser and nothing is stored.

Does the word counter count hyphenated words as one?

Yes. Words joined by a hyphen, like “well-known”, count as one word, the same as most word processors.

Do references count towards my word limit?

It depends on your course; many exclude the reference list. Check the assignment brief.

Does it work with Urdu, Arabic or Hindi text?

Yes. Words in any language separated by spaces are counted.
