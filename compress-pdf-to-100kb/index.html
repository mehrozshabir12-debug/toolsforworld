Compress PDF to 100KB

Reduce PDF size to under 100 KB in one step: choose a PDF and it is made just under 100 KB, keeping as much quality as possible. Ideal for online application forms and portals with a strict 100 KB limit.

How to compress a PDF to 100KB

  1. Choose your PDF.
  2. The target is already set to 100 KB. Change it if your form allows a different size.
  3. Press Compress PDF and download the smaller file.

How it works

Each page is re-saved as a compressed image. The tool tries several quality levels and keeps the sharpest one that still fits under 100 KB. Your file never leaves your device.

Tips

  • Scanned documents and PDFs full of photos shrink the most.
  • Fewer pages means better quality at 100 KB. Remove pages you don't need with Split PDF first.
  • Text in the result can no longer be selected or searched, which is fine for forms and scans.

What fits in 100 KB?

100 KB is a very tight limit. Roughly, it fits one or two scanned pages at readable quality, for example an ID card (front and back), a single certificate or a fee receipt.

  • Scan in black and white or greyscale if colour isn't required. It halves the size.
  • One page per file: if the portal lets you upload documents separately, split them with Split PDF.
  • Crop the scan to the document edges before making the PDF: table and background add size.

Questions

What if my PDF can't get under 100KB?

The tool shows the smallest size it could reach. Split the PDF into parts or remove pages, then try again.

Is my PDF uploaded?

No. Compression happens inside your browser, so the file stays on your device.

Can a 10-page PDF fit in 100 KB?

Rarely, unless it is plain text. For scanned pages, upload fewer pages or ask whether the portal allows a larger file.

Can every PDF get under 100 KB?

Short documents and single-page scans usually can. Long PDFs with many photos may need to be split into parts first.

Does this work for “below 100 KB”, “less than 100 KB” or “up to 100 KB” limits?

Yes. Under, below, less than and up to 100 KB all mean the same thing: the file must be 100 KB or smaller. The PDF is made just under 100 KB, so it passes any of these limits on upload forms.

Can I compress a PDF to 100 KB without losing quality?

Text-based PDFs (typed letters, forms, CVs) usually stay sharp, because text takes very little space. Scanned pages and photos have to be squeezed harder to reach 100 KB, so they can look softer. The tool always keeps the sharpest version that still fits, and you can check it before you submit.
