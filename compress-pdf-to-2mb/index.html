Compress PDF to 2MB

Reduce PDF size to under 2 MB in one step: choose a PDF and it is made just under 2 MB, keeping as much quality as possible. Ideal for job and university portals with a 2 MB upload limit.

How to compress a PDF to 2MB

  1. Choose your PDF.
  2. The target is already set to 2 MB. Change it if your form allows a different size.
  3. Press Compress PDF and download the smaller file.

How it works

Each page is re-saved as a compressed image. The tool tries several quality levels and keeps the sharpest one that still fits under 2 MB. Your file never leaves your device.

Tips

  • Scanned documents and PDFs full of photos shrink the most.
  • Fewer pages means better quality at 2 MB. Remove pages you don't need with Split PDF first.
  • Text in the result can no longer be selected or searched, which is fine for forms and scans.

What fits in 2 MB?

2 MB fits twenty to forty scanned pages, a thesis chapter with figures or a full set of supporting documents for an application.

  • Large scanned books or notes: split them into chapters with Split PDF if they still don't fit.
  • Figures and charts stay readable at 2 MB, but check small labels after compressing.
  • Text PDFs exported from Word rarely need compressing. Their size usually comes from embedded images.

Questions

What if my PDF can't get under 2MB?

The tool shows the smallest size it could reach. Split the PDF into parts or remove pages, then try again.

Is my PDF uploaded?

No. Compression happens inside your browser, so the file stays on your device.

How many pages can I fit in 2 MB?

It depends on the content. Plain text pages are tiny; colour photo pages are big. For typical phone scans, expect twenty to forty pages.

Does this work for “below 2 MB”, “less than 2 MB” or “up to 2 MB” limits?

Yes. Under, below, less than and up to 2 MB all mean the same thing: the file must be 2 MB or smaller. The PDF is made just under 2 MB, so it passes any of these limits on upload forms.

Can I compress a PDF to 2 MB without losing quality?

Usually yes. 2 MB is a generous limit, so most PDFs only lose detail you would not notice on screen. Very long scanned documents are the exception: split them or remove blank pages first.
