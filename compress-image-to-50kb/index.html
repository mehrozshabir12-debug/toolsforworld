Compress image to 50KB

Choose a photo and it is made just under 50 KB automatically, keeping as much quality as possible. Ideal for government and exam application forms.

How to compress a photo to 50KB

  1. Choose your photo (JPG, PNG or WebP).
  2. The target is already set to 50 KB. Change it if you need a different size.
  3. Press Compress and download the photo.

How it works

The tool tries many quality levels and picks the highest one that stays under 50 KB. If the photo is still too big at the lowest quality, it is scaled down a little at a time until it fits. Everything happens on your device, so your photo is never uploaded.

Tips

  • Crop away empty background first: less detail means better quality at 50 KB.
  • Most forms want JPG. Keep the output on JPG unless WebP is allowed.

How big is a 50 KB photo?

For a typical phone photo saved as JPG, 50 KB fits roughly about 400 × 500 pixels at good quality. Simple images (a signature, a face on a plain wall) fit more pixels; busy scenes with lots of detail fit fewer.

Government and exam portals often give both a size in cm (like 3.5 × 4.5 cm) and a KB range. Crop to the right shape first with Resize Image, then compress.

Where 50 KB limits appear

50 KB is one of the most common photo limits on government, exam and recruitment portals. Many forms ask for a photo between 20 and 50 KB together with a size in centimetres. Follow the size in cm first, then use this page to bring the file under 50 KB.

Photo and signature on the same form

Forms that want a photo under 50 KB usually also want a signature under 20 KB, and they're easy to swap by mistake. Name your files clearly before uploading, for example photo-50kb.jpg and signature-20kb.jpg.

If the portal shows your photo stretched or squashed after upload, the shape was wrong rather than the size. Check the width-to-height ratio it asks for (3.5 × 4.5 cm is a ratio of 7:9) and crop to that before compressing.

Questions

Is this the same as “resize image to 50 KB”?

Yes. When a form asks you to resize a photo to 50 KB, it means the file size. This tool lowers the file size to 50 KB and only reduces the width and height if it has to.

Will my photo still look good at 50KB?

For most photos, yes. The tool keeps the best quality that fits. Very large, detailed photos may be scaled down to fit.

Can I compress several photos to 50KB at once?

Yes. Choose several files and each one is compressed to the target size.

Will 50 KB be clear enough for an ID card?

Yes, for a cropped face photo. 50 KB keeps a passport-size photo sharp at the size it is printed on cards.

How do I check my photo's size in KB?

On Windows, right-click the photo and choose Properties. On a Mac, select it and press Cmd + I. On Android or iPhone, open the photo and look at its details or info. The tool also shows the new size after compressing.

Does this work for “below 50 KB”, “less than 50 KB” or “up to 50 KB” limits?

Yes. Under, below, less than and up to 50 KB all mean the photo must be 50 KB or smaller, and the result is made just under 50 KB. If a form gives a range instead, the result lands just under 50 KB, at the top of that range.
